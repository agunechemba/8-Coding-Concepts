Introduction

Hello, Little Coder!

A coder is someone who tells a computer what to do.

A coder is someone who tells a computer what to do. A computer is a machine that only does exactly what you say.

Guess what? You are already a coder! Every day you follow steps, keep them in order, fix mistakes, repeat things, make choices, break big jobs into little ones, give whole jobs a name, and keep things in labeled boxes — the eight big ideas in this book.

Let us learn the eight words for those superpowers, with simple games you can play with your class. No computer needed — just your brain, your friends, and your everyday routines. 🙌

Key Ideas

  • A coder tells a computer what to do.
  • A computer does exactly what you say — no more, no less.
  • You already do coder things every day.
  • Eight ideas, no computer required.

Try It Yourself

  1. What is a coder?
    Show answer

    Someone who tells a computer what to do.

  2. Do you need a computer to practice these eight ideas?
    Show answer

    No — every activity in this book works with just your brain, your friends, and your everyday routines.

  3. Name one thing you do every day that is like coding.
    Show answer

    Any routine with steps works — getting dressed, brushing your teeth, packing your backpack.

Part One · Coding Superpowers · Chapter 1

Algorithm 🥪

Steps in order to get something done.

An algorithm is just a list of steps you do in order to finish a job. It is like a recipe: if you skip a step, or do it in the wrong order, the job does not work.

Try it in your head: Can you put jelly on bread before you open the jar? Nope! The jar is closed! That is why the steps and their order both matter.

Making a peanut butter and jelly sandwich is a perfect everyday example — the steps are your algorithm:

  1. Open the bread bag 🍞
  2. Take out two slices
  3. Get the peanut butter 🥜
  4. Spread it
  5. Get the jelly 🍓
  6. Spread it
  7. Put the slices together

You already follow algorithms every day — getting dressed, brushing your teeth, feeding a pet. The trick is that every step has to come in the right order.

Key Ideas

  • An algorithm is a list of steps in order.
  • The steps must be complete — if you skip one, the job breaks.
  • The order matters — jelly before opening the jar just does not work.
  • You already follow algorithms every day.

Try It Yourself

  1. What is an algorithm?
    Show answer

    A list of steps you follow in order to finish a job. 📋

  2. What happens if you forget a step, like opening the jelly jar?
    Show answer

    The whole thing breaks — you cannot spread jelly from a closed jar. 🍓

  3. Tell an algorithm for washing your hands. How many steps does it have?
    Show answer

    Any answer with steps in order works — e.g., turn on the water, get soap, rub, rinse, dry. Count them together!

Part One · Coding Superpowers · Chapter 2

Sequence 📖

The order of the steps matters.

Sequence means the order of things. If you change the order, you change the story. Would you put on your shoes before your socks? That would be silly! The order is the code — change it and the code does something different.

Stories have a sequence too: a beginning, a middle, and an end. If you mix it up and start with the end, it does not make sense — like eating dessert before dinner. 🍰

Algorithms teach you the steps; sequence reminds you that the order of those steps is just as important as the steps themselves.

Key Ideas

  • Sequence is the order of the steps.
  • Change the order and the result changes.
  • Stories have sequence too — beginning, middle, end.
  • Socks before shoes, always.

Try It Yourself

  1. What does "sequence" mean?
    Show answer

    The order of the steps — what comes first, next, and last. 🔢

  2. What happens to a story if the order is wrong?
    Show answer

    It gets confusing and silly — it does not make sense anymore. 🤪

  3. Shoes or socks first? Why?
    Show answer

    Socks first, then shoes! Otherwise your socks will not fit right. 🧦👟

Part One · Coding Superpowers · Chapter 3

Debugging 🐛

Finding a mistake and fixing it.

A bug is a mistake in your steps. Debugging means finding the bug and fixing it.

Bugs are not bad. Every coder makes them — even grown-up coders who build rockets. When something goes wrong, you do not get mad. You look for the bug, fix it, and try again. That is the superpower.

Here is an everyday bug: you put your shoes on the wrong feet. Ouch — that feels funny! You look down and spot the mistake (finding the bug), swap the shoes (fixing the bug), and now they feel right (bug gone). Other everyday bugs: your block tower falls down 🧱, your zipper gets stuck 🤐, a puzzle piece does not fit 🧩. You already know how to debug — now you know the word.

Key Ideas

  • A bug is a mistake in the steps.
  • Debugging is finding and fixing the bug.
  • Mistakes are normal — even rocket scientists make them.
  • The move is: look, fix, try again.

Try It Yourself

  1. What is a bug?
    Show answer

    A mistake in the steps. 🐛

  2. Is it bad to make a mistake?
    Show answer

    No! Everybody makes bugs — even grown-up coders. Mistakes help us learn. 💪

  3. Tell about a time you fixed a bug today.
    Show answer

    Any answer works — a stuck zipper, a fallen tower, a mixed-up sock. Praise the fixing, not just the result. 🌟

Part One · Coding Superpowers · Chapter 4

Loop 🔁

Doing the same thing again and again until you stop.

A loop is when you do something over and over. It keeps going until something tells it to stop.

Like a song you sing again and again. Or turning the pages of a book — flip, flip, flip — until you reach the very last page. That is your stopping point.

Your school day is full of loops: walking to specials class, eating a sandwich, taking a shower, the calendar routine every morning, the clean-up song at the end of centers. Each one happens again and again — just like a loop in a computer.

Key Ideas

  • A loop repeats the same steps.
  • Every loop needs a stopping condition.
  • Your day is full of loops — eating, walking, singing.
  • The stopping condition is what ends the loop.

Try It Yourself

  1. What is a loop?
    Show answer

    Doing the same thing again and again until something tells you to stop. 🔁

  2. Name one loop you do every single day.
    Show answer

    Brushing teeth, eating breakfast, singing the clean-up song — lots of answers work! 🪥

  3. What is a "stopping condition"?
    Show answer

    The thing that makes the loop stop — like the bell ringing or the song ending. 🛑

Part One · Coding Superpowers · Chapter 5

Branch (If / Then) 🌳

If this happens, then I do that.

A branch is a choice. It is an "if / then" thought:

IF it is raining 🌧️, THEN I bring my umbrella ☂️.
IF I am hungry 🍎, THEN I eat a snack.

It is like a path that splits into two — you pick one. You do this all day long, and you are really good at it.

Think about a normal school day. What if it is raining at recess — what happens then? We stay inside! What if you forgot your lunchbox — what do you do? We ask for help! Those "what if" questions are branching statements.

Key Ideas

  • A branch is an "if / then" choice.
  • The path splits into two — you pick one.
  • You make branching decisions all day.
  • "What if" questions create branches.

Try It Yourself

  1. What does "if / then" mean?
    Show answer

    It means making a choice: IF something happens, THEN I do something. 🌳

  2. Finish this: "IF I am sleepy, THEN…"
    Show answer

    …THEN I go to sleep! 😴 (Any sensible "then" works.)

  3. Make up your own "if / then" about school.
    Show answer

    For example: "IF the bell rings, THEN we line up." Great branching logic! 🔔

Part One · Coding Superpowers · Chapter 6

Decomposition 🧩

Break a big job into little jobs.

Decomposition is a big word that means breaking a big job into small pieces. Small pieces are much easier to do.

A whole pizza is too big to eat in one bite — so we cut it into slices. That is decomposition. Big job → little steps → all done.

A grown-up says, "Just tie your shoe." But your shoe has lots of hidden little steps:

  1. Cross the laces 👟
  2. Make a loop 🌀
  3. Wrap the other lace around
  4. Pull it through
  5. Tighten it ✨

Breaking "tie your shoe" into small pieces is decomposition.

Key Ideas

  • Decomposition means breaking a big job into small pieces.
  • Small pieces are easier than one big job.
  • A pizza slice is decomposition of the pizza.
  • Even "easy" jobs like shoe-tying have hidden steps.

Try It Yourself

  1. What does "decomposition" mean in kid words?
    Show answer

    Breaking a big job into little, easy pieces. 🧩

  2. Why is a big job easier when you break it up?
    Show answer

    Because each little piece is small and easy to do — you do not have to do it all at once! 😊

  3. Break "getting ready for bed" into at least three little steps.
    Show answer

    For example: put on pajamas 🩳, brush teeth 🪥, read a story 📚. Any three in order work!

Part One · Coding Superpowers · Chapter 7

Function 🎁

One name for a whole bunch of steps.

A function is a bunch of steps with one name. You say the name, and all the steps happen — like magic.

Think of it as a present with a label on it. The label is short, but there is a whole lot inside. You do not have to say every step — you just say the name.

Your teacher says one little word: "Clean up!" 🧹 But that one word really means:

  1. Stop playing 🛑
  2. Put the toys in the bins 🧸
  3. Push in the chairs 🪑
  4. Sit on the rug 🌈

Four steps! One name! That is a function. Other magic names at school: "Line up!", "Wash hands!", "Get your coat!" — all short names for whole batches of steps.

Key Ideas

  • A function is a group of steps with a single name.
  • Say the name and all the steps happen.
  • "Clean up!", "Line up!", "Wash hands!" are functions.
  • Functions save time — one word instead of many.

Try It Yourself

  1. What is a function?
    Show answer

    A bunch of steps with one name. Say the name and all the steps happen. 🎁

  2. When your teacher says "Clean up!", what steps does that one name really mean?
    Show answer

    Stop playing, put toys away, push in chairs, sit on the rug — and maybe more! 🧹

  3. Can you make up a name for a bunch of steps? What does your name mean?
    Show answer

    Any invented name with 2–3 agreed steps works. Try it as a class! 🍌

Part One · Coding Superpowers · Chapter 8

Variables 📦

A named box that holds something.

A variable is a named box that holds something for you. The name stays the same, but what is inside can change.

Think of your cubby at school. It has your name on it. On Monday you put your coat inside. On Tuesday you put your hat inside. The name on the cubby never changes — but the thing inside it does. That is a variable!

Or think of a jar labeled "snacks" 🍪. Today it might hold apple slices. Tomorrow it might hold crackers. You just say "the snack jar" and everyone knows exactly which jar you mean.

Other everyday variables: your lunchbox (holds lunch — but a different lunch each day), your piggy bank 🐷 (holds coins — but the amount keeps changing), and "today's weather" (sunny 🌞 one day, rainy 🌧️ the next, but always called "today's weather").

Key Ideas

  • A variable is a named box that holds something.
  • The name stays the same; what is inside can change.
  • Labels help us find things fast.
  • Your cubby, lunchbox, and piggy bank are like variables.

Try It Yourself

  1. What is a variable?
    Show answer

    A named box that holds something. 📦

  2. Can what is inside a variable change?
    Show answer

    Yes! The name stays the same, but what is inside can change. 🔄

  3. Name something at home that is like a variable.
    Show answer

    Any labeled container works — a cookie jar, your cubby, a pencil case, a piggy bank. 🏷️
